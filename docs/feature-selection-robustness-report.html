Team decision report · 30 August 2026

Keep the three-feature core. Test the six fragile features—do not promote them wholesale.

A teammate-facing interpretation of the current AUPRC feature laboratory, with transform failure evidence, selection rules, and exact residual-kurtosis implementation references.

Executive decision

Use the three retained features as the current compact candidate core: residual kurtosis, Haar high-band kurtosis, and Fourier phase-neighbor coherence. Keep the other six in the experimental matrix because they expose important failure modes, but do not automatically add them to the deployed detector.

Core candidate set
3 retained signals from complementary representations
residual_kurtosiswavelet_hf_kurtosisphase_neighbor_coherence

All three cleared the current clean, powered-group, and official-transform screens without a powered generator direction reversal.

Do not confuse two claims
“Large performance drop” is not “strong detector feature.”

The six examples were chosen because blur, resize, or JPEG damaged their separation. Two are currently marked shortcut and four discard.

The core is not the final answer either. Residual kurtosis and wavelet high-frequency kurtosis are strongly redundant on clean discovery images (Spearman ρ = 0.887). Compare the three-feature core against a two-feature version before locking the architecture. Phase-neighbor coherence is the most representation-diverse member.

Why “all six” is risky: this block is internally redundant. On clean discovery images, |ρ| is 0.968 for residual-neighbor correlation versus DCT high-AC ratio, 0.938 for DCT high-AC ratio versus FFT radial slope, and 0.902 for wavelet level-2 ratio versus residual-neighbor correlation. Adding all six increases shortcut capacity much more than independent evidence.

Does the hackathon warning show up in our data?

Yes. Severe blur and resize produce the largest, most consistent losses in high-frequency, local-correlation, DCT, and multiscale features. JPEG damage is clearest in the native view. Recommended feature-selection experiment

Tier A

Compact core

Compare all 3 retained features with residual_kurtosis + phase_neighbor_coherence to test kurtosis redundancy.

→
Tier B

One specialist at a time

Add one family representative per run—never the full correlated block first.

→
Tier C

Nuisance controls

Use codec/resize sensors to audit or calibrate reliability, not as direct AIGC votes unless locked evaluation proves value.

  1. Make selection only inside discovery data using nested, group-aware validation. Do not tune on current confirmation rows.
  2. Compare: best single feature; core-2; core-3; core-3 plus each specialist; and core-3 plus a regularized family set.
  3. Train with official transforms and compositions while preserving clean examples. Include transform order and severity sweeps rather than only a Cartesian explosion.
  4. Score clean AUPRC, mean official-transform AUPRC, worst-condition normalized AUPRC, leave-one-dataset/generator results, direction reversals, and calibration.
  5. Keep an addition only if it improves a locked robustness/generalization target across seeds without a material nuisance correlation or subgroup reversal.
  6. After freezing the subset and model, evaluate once on untouched confirmation and then on expanded datasets.
Practical architecture: a small regularized engineered-feature branch is reasonable as one branch of the final system, but should complement a generator-general semantic/representation branch. The workshop’s hybrid suggestion is guidance, not a requirement.

Residual kurtosis: exact implementation and notebook status

The scalar implementation is in the reusable Python package, not duplicated inline in the marimo notebook. That gives another agent one authoritative implementation instead of drifting copies.

Y = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B        # RGB normalized to [0, 1]
S = GaussianFilter(Y, sigma=1, mode="reflect")
E = Y - S
residual_kurtosis = mean((E - mean(E))^4) / mean((E - mean(E))^2)^2 - 3
